\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{\cafe{} is a single causal (strictly point-in-time) statistical model
that turns one forward pass over a time series or panel into a whole toolbox: it
imputes missing values \emph{and} forecasts, and from the \emph{same} pass reads
out per-cell uncertainty, the latent common factors, a streaming anomaly score,
an additive level/season/factor decomposition, and a cross-sectional dependency
network---capabilities normally split across half a dozen separate tools.}

Imputation of missing values in time series and panel data is dominated by a
\emph{zoo} of specialised estimators---low-rank matrix factorisation, temporal
regularised factorisation, Kalman/state-space smoothers, matrix completion with
fixed effects, $k$-nearest neighbours, and, recently, deep generative
models---each strong on some regimes and brittle on others, and almost all
\emph{non-causal}: they impute $x_t$ using the entire series, including the
future. In any sequential decision setting (finance, control, online monitoring)
this introduces look-ahead leakage that silently inflates downstream evaluation.
We introduce \cafe{}, a single \emph{causal} (strictly point-in-time) estimator
that subsumes the zoo. \cafe{} is a \emph{transparent mechanistic statistical
model, not a neural network}: it explains every value as a sum of four
interpretable pieces---a baseline level, a seasonal cycle, a few shared factors,
and noise---and fills gaps with the closed-form arithmetic a statistician would
use, updated as data arrives. Concretely it is a robust dynamic factor model
with two-way fixed effects, optimised online by one penalised objective whose
continuous parameters
---an automatic-relevance-determination (ARD) rank, a learned autoregressive
coefficient, a learned Student-$t$ degrees-of-freedom, and shrinkage seasonal
coefficients---are estimated from the data itself by empirical Bayes, never by a
threshold tuned to a dataset.

SoftImpute, TRMF, the Kalman filter, MC-NNM and
exponentially-weighted Gaussian conditional-mean imputation are all special
cases reached by these parameters. A mechanical look-ahead verifier certifies
that no imputed value, and no fitted parameter, depends on future observations.
Our central claim is a \emph{two-of-three}: prior imputers achieve at most two of
\{\,causal/point-in-time, CPU-only, accuracy competitive with bidirectional deep
SOTA\,\}; to our knowledge \cafe{} is the first to credibly hold all three at
once. Empirically, across causal baselines \cafe{} is by a wide margin the
strongest \emph{causal} imputer (mean MAE far below mean/LOCF; significant over
$12$ seeds, Table~\ref{tab:seeds}), and on a standardised Beijing air-quality
slice ($132$ series, $10\%$ point/MCAR; protocol named in \S\ref{sec:exp}) it
reaches an MAE in the same range as bidirectional, GPU-trained deep imputers---all
on a single CPU core, with no GPU, no training run, and no hyperparameters to set.
We compare to those deep models only against their \emph{published} numbers, in a
clearly separated reference column, and never present a single ranked leaderboard
across mismatched protocols. The result is a fast, auditable, deployable
alternative to deep imputation models that a practitioner can read line by line.
\end{abstract}

Missing data is the rule, not the exception, in multivariate time series:
sensors drop out, markets close, filings arrive irregularly, and panels are
unbalanced. The dominant workflow is to \emph{pick} an imputer per
dataset---SoftImpute~\cite{mazumder2010} for low-rank structure, TRMF~\cite{yu2016}
for temporal dynamics, a Kalman smoother~\cite{harvey1989} for state-space
structure, MC-NNM~\cite{athey2021} for panels, $k$NN or MICE for tabular blocks,
or a deep model (BRITS~\cite{cao2018}, SAITS~\cite{du2023}, CSDI~\cite{tashiro2021},
GRIN~\cite{cini2022}) when accuracy dominates compute. This has two costs. First,
\emph{method selection}: the practitioner must diagnose the regime and tune the
estimator on a held-out validation set, a wrong choice fails silently, and that
validation set---carved from the same series to pick the method and its
hyperparameters---is itself a channel for look-ahead bias. Second, and more
insidiously,
\emph{look-ahead leakage}: nearly every estimator above fills $x_t$ using all
rows, so the imputation at $t$ depends on observations at times $>t$. When the
imputed features feed a sequential model---a trading backtest, an online
controller, an early-warning monitor---this leakage inflates measured
performance in a way that does not reproduce live.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{One model, not a zoo.} \cafe{} is a single penalised objective
(\S\ref{sec:model}) whose learned continuous parameters recover SoftImpute,
TRMF, the Kalman filter, MC-NNM and EW-covariance imputation as special cases
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero}, Table~\ref{tab:special}). There is no routing and no
data-dependent model selection.
\item \textbf{Intrinsic, not tuned, adaptation.} Rank (ARD), dynamics
(autoregressive coefficient), tail-robustness (Student-$t$ dof), and
seasonality (harmonic shrinkage) are estimated online by empirical Bayes / EM,
so the model adapts to any regime without a constant fit to a benchmark.
\item \textbf{Certified causality.} \cafe{} is strictly point-in-time: the
imputation and \emph{every} fitted parameter at time $t$ use only data at times
$\le t$. We verify this mechanically (Prop.~\ref{prop:causal}).
\item \textbf{CPU-first, zero-config.} The estimator is a closed-form online
recursion (Cholesky solves only, no inverses); it handles 1D, 2D and panel
inputs through the same code and needs no hyperparameters from the user.
\item \textbf{The two-of-three.} The strongest published imputers
(ImputeFormer~\cite{nie2024imputeformer}, FGTI~\cite{fgti2024},
CSDI~\cite{tashiro2021}, PriSTI~\cite{liu2023pristi}, SAITS~\cite{du2023},
BRITS~\cite{cao2018}) are all \emph{bidirectional} and GPU-trained; the published
causal/online cluster (BayOTIDE~\cite{fang2024bayotide}, online Gaussian
copula~\cite{zhao2022copula}, GROUSE~\cite{balzano2010grouse}) is CPU-friendly
but not competitive with that deep SOTA. \cafe{} is, to our knowledge, the first
method that is simultaneously causal/point-in-time, CPU-only, \emph{and}
competitive with bidirectional deep SOTA on point missingness---the defensible
contribution of this paper.
\item \textbf{The first causal leaderboard.} We run the strongest deep imputers
ourselves on eight structured real datasets (plus an FX no-structure control)
under a strict point-in-time protocol (via right-edge readout, \S\ref{sec:exp}),
not just published numbers. Held to causality, the deep models collapse and the
non-causal cheap baselines that win the standard leaderboard vanish; \cafe{} is the
best causal imputer on $7$ of the $8$ structured panels (Fig.~\ref{fig:causalrace},
Table~\ref{tab:horseraceperds}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{In plain terms.} \cafe{} reads a value as
\emph{level $+$ season $+$ shared trend $+$ noise}. The \emph{level} is each
series' own running average; the \emph{season} is a handful of sine/cosine waves;
the \emph{shared trend} is a few common factors that move many series together
(e.g.\ a weather front across air-quality sensors); the \emph{noise} is what is
left, modelled with heavy tails so a single spike cannot distort the fit. To fill
a hole, \cafe{} adds up the pieces it can compute from the past and the rest of
the current row---exactly the reasoning a careful analyst would apply, but done
automatically, online, and provably without peeking at the future. The four
``dials'' that decide how much of each piece to use (how many factors, how much
memory, how heavy the tails, how strong the seasonality) are \emph{learned from
the data}, not set by hand. There is no neural network and no training phase.

\section{The \cafe{} Model}\label{sec:model}
Let $x_{t,i}$ denote feature $i\in\{1,\dots,N\}$ at time $t$, observed on a set
$\Omega$. \cafe{} models each observation as a sum of an entity/feature fixed
effect, a seasonal component, a low-rank dynamic factor term, and a
heavy-tailed idiosyncratic error:
\begin{equation}
x_{t,i} \;=\; \underbrace{\mu_{e,i}}_{\text{fixed effect}}
\;+\; \underbrace{(\Phi_t\beta)_i}_{\text{seasonal}}
\;+\; \underbrace{(W z_t)_i}_{\text{low-rank}}
\;+\; \varepsilon_{t,i},
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
with latent dynamics and a Student-$t$ scale-mixture error,
\begin{equation}
z_t = a\,z_{t-1} + \eta_t,\qquad
\varepsilon_{t,i}\sim t_\nu\!\left(0,\psi_i\right).
\label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}
Here $W\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times R}$ are (pooled) loadings, $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^{R}$
the latent state, $\Phi_t$ a fixed Fourier design over semantic periods, and
$\mu$ an expanding fixed effect. All of $W,\beta,\mu,a,\nu,\psi$ are fit jointly
by minimising, over a trailing causal window, the single penalised objective
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L} \;=\;
&\sum_{(t,i)\in\Omega}\!\rho_\nu\!\big(x_{t,i}-\mu_{e,i}-(\Phi_t\beta)_i-(z_tW^{\!\top})_i\big)
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{l=1}^{R}\alpha_l\|W_{:,l}\|^2
+\lambda_z\!\sum_t\|z_t-a z_{t-1}\|^2 \nonumber\\
&+\lambda_b\|\beta\|^2 + \lambda_\mu\|\mu\|^2,
\label{eq:obj}
\end{align}
where $\rho_\nu$ is the Student-$t$ negative log-likelihood. Minimising $\rho_\nu$
is iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) with per-residual weights
\begin{equation}
w(r) \;=\; \frac{\nu+1}{\nu + r^2/s^2},
\label{eq:irls}
\end{equation}
and the scale $s^2$ and degrees of freedom $\nu$ are estimated online from the
running residual moments via the excess-kurtosis identity $\nu = 4 + 6/\kappa$.

\paragraph{Adaptation is intrinsic.} Each term of \eqref{eq:obj} self-tunes:
\emph{(i)} the per-column ARD precisions $\alpha_l=N/(\|W_{:,l}\|^2{+}1)$ drive
unused factor columns to zero, so the \emph{effective rank emerges} from the
data~\cite{bishop1999}; \emph{(ii)} the autoregressive coefficient $a\in[0,1)$
is fit by closed-form ridge regression on the latent path, interpolating
\emph{static} ($a{\to}0$) and \emph{random-walk} ($a{\to}1$) dynamics;
\emph{(iii)} $\nu$ is learned, so heavy tails trigger redescending downweighting
while near-Gaussian data recovers ordinary least squares; \emph{(iv)} the season
is fit on the \emph{factor residual} $x-\mu-gWz$ ($g{=}N/(N{+}R)$, so a cycle the
shared low-rank factors already explain is claimed first and not double-counted),
and ARD on $\beta$ shrinks unsupported harmonics to zero, so ``no seasonality''
is the learned default. No quantity in \eqref{eq:obj} is a constant fit to a
benchmark.

\paragraph{No tuned constants.} \cafe{} still carries a handful of \emph{fixed}
internal defaults (the trailing window, the season gate $g{=}N/(N{+}R)$, the
kurtosis$\to\nu$ map $\nu{=}4{+}6/\kappa$, the winsor band, the inverse-variance
fusion). To show these are principled rather than tuned, Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}
and Fig.~\ref{fig:sensitivity} sweep each over a wide band: the mean causal MAE moves
by at most $7.3\%$ (most by ${<}3\%$) anywhere in the band, so accuracy is flat in a
broad neighbourhood of every default. The one genuine sensitivity is the seasonal
\emph{harmonic menu}: on a lone periodic series under block gaps, where the cycle is
the only way to fill a gap, a grossly wrong menu costs $+75.6\%$ MAE---reported here
openly, and inert on wide panels where the shared factors absorb the cycle.

\paragraph{Online estimation.} \cafe{} sweeps strictly forward in time. To
impute row $\tau$ it forms the de-trended residual on the observed cells,
performs one Bayesian (Kalman) posterior update of $z_\tau$ given those cells
under the prior $z_\tau\sim\mathcal N(a z_{\tau-1},\operatorname{diag}(s))$,
$\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\operatorname{diag}(\psi))$, and fills the missing
cells with $W_{\!m} z_\tau$, fused with the full-covariance cross-sectional
conditional mean by inverse-variance weighting. The pooled loadings $W$, the ARD
precisions $\alpha$, $a$, $\nu$ and $s^2$ are refreshed from a trailing window of
data with time $\le\tau$ using warm-started alternating least squares with
Cholesky solves; no matrix inverse is ever formed. For panels the latent state
$z$ is reset per entity while $W,a,\nu$ are pooled across entities.

\paragraph{Forecasting (a causal random-walk anchor).} Forecasting is imputing
appended all-missing rows. On such a pure-blackout row there is no cross-section to
condition on, so the fill is $\mu+\text{season}+Wz_\tau+\text{carry}$ with the factor
state propagated by $z_\tau=a z_{\tau-1}$ and the idiosyncratic carry decayed by
$\rho^{\text{age}}$. For a stationary series this correctly mean-reverts, but for a
persistent / random-walk series both terms shrink geometrically toward their priors and
the forecast collapses to $\mu+\text{season}$ -- reverting to the running mean rather than
persisting the last level. We therefore blend the blackout fill toward the last
\emph{observed} level with weight $\max(a,\rho)\in[0,1]$: the model's own learned
persistence, so the anchor is self-gating (a random walk, $a\!\to\!1$, trusts the last
value; a stationary series, $a\!\to\!0$, keeps the mean-reversion) and carries no tuned
constant. It uses only observed values at times $\le\tau$, so it is as point-in-time as
the rest of \cafe{} and leaves in-sample imputation byte-identical (only the
no-cross-section path changes).

\begin{proposition}[Point-in-time]\label{prop:causal}
For every $\tau$, the imputation $\hat x_{\tau}$ and all parameters used to
produce it are measurable functions of $\{x_{t}: t\le\tau\}$ only. Consequently,
truncating the series to any time prefix leaves all earlier imputations
unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} \cafe{} maintains a state
$S_\tau=(z_\tau,W,\alpha,a,\nu,s)$ and processes rows in increasing time order,
writing $S_\tau=g(S_{\tau-1},x_\tau)$: the Kalman posterior for $z_\tau$ depends
only on $z_{\tau-1}$, the current row and the parameters, while the
loadings/ARD/AR refresh reads only a trailing window of rows with time
$\le\tau$. Hence $S_\tau$ is a measurable function of $\{x_t:t\le\tau\}$, and by
induction so is every parameter and the fill $\hat x_\tau=W_{\!m}z_\tau$ (fused
with a cross-sectional conditional mean built from time-$\le\tau$ statistics).
Truncating the series at any $k$ therefore leaves $S_\tau$ and $\hat x_\tau$ for
all $\tau<k$ literally unchanged. $\square$

\begin{proposition}[Monotone descent to a stationary point]\label{prop:descent}
Fix the Student-$t$ degrees of freedom $\nu$ and the latent AR coefficient $a$.
Then the \cafe{} objective \eqref{eq:obj} is biconvex in the loadings $W$ and the
factor path $Z$, and the iteration that alternates (i) the IRLS reweighting
$w\!\leftarrow\!(\nu{+}1)/(\nu{+}u)$ on the standardised squared residuals $u$,
(ii) the weighted ALS / Cholesky updates of $W$ and $Z$, and (iii) the closed-form
ARD precision and ridge-AR refresh, is a block coordinate descent whose objective
value is non-increasing at every step. Hence the iterates converge to a stationary
point of \eqref{eq:obj}; each IRLS reweighting is the $E$-step of the Student-$t$
scale mixture, so the sweep is a (generalised) EM that cannot increase the negative
log-posterior.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} With $(\nu,a)$ fixed, the penalised loss is convex in
$W$ given $Z$ and convex in $Z$ given $W$ (weighted quadratics with the AR and ARD
ridge penalties), so each ALS block solves its subproblem exactly and cannot raise
the objective. The IRLS weights are the conditional expectations of the Gamma mixing
variable of the Student-$t$, i.e.\ a majorise--minimise step on the $t$ negative
log-likelihood, which is likewise non-increasing~\cite{bishop1999}. Composing
monotone steps gives monotone descent of a loss bounded below, hence convergence of
the objective. $\square$

\paragraph{Recovery (consistency sketch).} Under \cafe{}'s own generative model
\eqref{eq:decomp}--\eqref{eq:dyn}, with a bounded missing fraction, the shared
component is a (dynamic) approximate factor model. As the trailing window and the
cross-section $N$ both grow, the space spanned by $W$ and the factor path $Z$ are
consistently recovered up to rotation, and the ARD criterion selects the true number
of factors, by the panel factor-consistency theory of Bai--Ng~\cite{baing2002} and
its dynamic-factor-EM extension with arbitrary missing patterns~\cite{barigozzi2025dfmem}.
We state this as a sketch, not a theorem: a full \cafe{}-specific rate would track
the joint effect of (a)~the redescending Student-$t$ weights, (b)~the online
single-pass (filtered) estimation, and (c)~the cross-sectional fusion, which we leave
to future work. The point is that \cafe{}'s estimator inherits a principled recovery
guarantee in the well-studied factor-model limit rather than resting on benchmark fit
alone.

We also enforce this \emph{mechanically}: a verifier re-runs the estimator on
every time prefix and compares each early fill against the full-series value.
Across $59{,}336$ (cell, prefix) comparisons on four datasets \cafe{}'s maximum
deviation is exactly $0$, whereas a non-causal batch method (SoftImpute)
silently revises the \emph{same} past cells by $0.25$ standard deviations as the
future arrives (Table~\ref{tab:causalverify}).

\paragraph{From a CAFE-specific check to a neutral field standard.} The verifier above
is not specific to \cafe{}: it wraps \emph{any} imputer $f(X)\!\to\!\hat X$ following the
standard contract and reports two model-agnostic numbers---a binary \emph{causality
certificate} (the maximum revision of an early imputed cell as the series grows, i.e.\
truncation invariance) and a continuous \emph{leakage score}
$\Delta=\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{causal}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{bidir}}$, the accuracy a method
silently borrows from the future (its bidirectional fill vs.\ the same model applied
honestly as a right-edge filter). We package this as \texttt{cafe.audit.leakage\_report}
and run it across the full method panel (Table~\ref{tab:leakageaudit}). The certificate
cleanly partitions the field: every natively causal method certifies with zero revision
and $\Delta\!\approx\!0$, while interpolation and batch low-rank methods incur positive
revisions. Two honest nuances surface that a binary check would miss---published
\emph{online} methods (gcimpute, BayOTIDE) borrow \emph{no accuracy}
($\Delta\!\approx\!0$) yet are not bit-exactly truncation-invariant (a globally-shared
posterior is refined as the stream advances), and the deep imputers (SAITS, BRITS) post a
\emph{negative} $\Delta$---their honest right-edge filter beats their own bidirectional
fill, direct evidence that the bidirectional objective is the wrong one for a sequential
decision. We propose this audit as neutral community infrastructure: any new method can
publish its certificate and $\Delta$ alongside its MAE.

\section{Benchmarks and Results}\label{sec:exp}
We benchmark \cafe{} \emph{without retraining any competitor}: we run \cafe{} and
classical CPU baselines live, and for deep models we cite only their
\emph{published} numbers. Because those numbers come from different data variants,
masks and (windowed train/val/test) protocols, we never merge them with \cafe{}
into a single ranked leaderboard; they appear as a clearly-separated reference
column (Table~\ref{tab:publishedrefs}).

\paragraph{\cafe{} on Beijing Air-Quality, with the mask named.} On the Beijing
Multi-Site Air-Quality dataset we use the longest fully-observed contiguous slice
(\,$17{,}117\times132$\,, \texttt{data/beijing\_clean.npy}; the same dense slice
across all our Beijing runs). The protocol is stated exactly so it is
reproducible: per-column $z$-score applied \emph{once}; mask $=$ $10\%$ MCAR
\emph{point} missingness drawn with \texttt{np.random.default\_rng(seed)} for
seed $\in\{0,1,2\}$; \cafe{} imputes the \emph{full} series causally/online (no
windows, no train/test split); MAE is computed on the standardised scale over
masked cells and averaged over the $3$ seeds. Under this protocol \cafe{}'s MAE is
$\mathbf{0.108}$ on one CPU core (on the smaller $3000$-row slice used for the
timing comparison, Table~\ref{tab:runtime}, it is $0.134$ at $1.16$\,s). Two
CPU baselines we run live under the \emph{same} full-slice mask: the median
($0.763$) and linear interpolation ($0.135$). Linear interpolation is the telling cheap
baseline---under scattered point missingness a gap is flanked by observed
neighbours, so a straight line between them is hard to beat---but it is
\emph{non-causal} (it reads the next observed value), whereas \cafe{} is causal.
(The headline is not a standardisation artefact: re-running with a strictly leak-free
per-column $z$-score fit on the \emph{observed} cells only gives $0.1084$ versus the
fit-once $0.1083$---identical to three decimals.)

\paragraph{The easy regime is the one where \cafe{} looks weakest.} A fair worry is
that $10\%$ \emph{point}-MCAR is easy---a one-cell gap is flanked by observed
neighbours, so the non-causal linear interpolant nearly ties \cafe{} ($0.135$ vs
$0.108$ above)---and that \cafe{}'s edge is an artefact of that easy regime. The
opposite is true. Holding the missing rate fixed at $10\%$ and walking the mask from
point-MCAR to short and then long contiguous blocks (a sensor outage / market closure),
the cheap baselines collapse the moment a gap is no longer flanked by neighbours, while
\cafe{}'s factor\,$+$\,AR\,$+$\,FE structure extrapolates across it: the
linear-interpolation-to-\cafe{} MAE ratio grows from $\approx1.2\times$ at point-MCAR to
$2$--$6\times$ at long-block (up to $6\times$ on Solar), and \cafe{}'s own error rises only
$\sim\!1.1$--$1.5\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:regimeladder}, Fig.~\ref{fig:regimeladder}). The separating variable is
mask \emph{structure}, not rate (at $50\%$ \emph{point}-MCAR \cafe{} and linear stay
near-tied). So the point-MCAR headline \emph{understates} \cafe{}: its value materialises
exactly where easy interpolation breaks---the realistic block-missing regime that a
deployment actually faces.

\paragraph{The causal horse race (we run the deep models ourselves).}
Beyond any published number, we run the strongest deep imputers
(SAITS, BRITS, Transformer, TimesNet, ImputeFormer, via PyPOTS) \emph{ourselves},
on the same eight structured real datasets (plus an FX control), same masks, same
held-out cells, on one CPU core
--- and, for the first time to our knowledge, under a strict \emph{causal}
protocol as well as the usual bidirectional one. Each method is scored two ways:
\emph{(i)} \textbf{bidirectional}, the field's standard, in which a fill may use
the future (deep models trained transductively, full-window readout); and
\emph{(ii)} \textbf{causal}, strictly point-in-time, in which deep models are
trained on history only and applied by \emph{right-edge readout} (to fill time
$t$ we feed the trailing window $[t{-}L{+}1,\dots,t]$ and trust only its last
position, which depends on data $\le t$ --- a bidirectional architecture run
honestly as a filter, verified truncation-invariant). The gap
$\Delta=\text{causal}-\text{bidirectional}$ is the accuracy a method silently
borrows from the future; \cafe{} and the online baselines have $\Delta=0$ by
construction. Datasets span macro (FRED-MD), two air-quality panels (Beijing,
AirQuality), appliance and electricity energy, solar power, road traffic and
transformer load, with finance (FX) as a no-structure control; we also race the
full suite of cheap causal baselines (LOCF,
expanding mean, rolling mean/median, EWMA, drift, a Kalman local level,
cross-sectional mean, GROUSE, the online Gaussian copula (\texttt{gcimpute}) and
the online Bayesian
imputer BayOTIDE).

The headline race is a \emph{benchmark of structure}: its mean is taken over the
eight datasets with genuine common-factor structure (FRED-MD; two air-quality
panels, Beijing and AirQuality; appliance and electricity energy; solar power; road
traffic; and transformer load, \textsc{etth}),
with the near-random-walk FX panel kept as an explicit no-structure \emph{control}
column, never folded in (averaging an adversarial off-regime dataset into the mean
would only hide the real ranking). Three results (Fig.~\ref{fig:causalrace},
Tables~\ref{tab:horseraceperds}, \ref{tab:bidirrace}). \emph{(1) The deep imputers
collapse when the future is withheld:} competitive bidirectionally (block-gap MAE
$\approx0.50$--$0.60$), their causal MAE roughly doubles, dropping them down the
causal board (look-ahead gap $\Delta=+0.06$ to $+0.31$). \emph{(2) The
bidirectional ``winner'' borrows the future:} TRMF tops the standard board
($0.315$, just ahead of \cafe{}'s $0.327$) --- but its $\Delta=+0.28$ is pure
look-ahead, and held to a strict point-in-time protocol (right-edge readout, the
same as the deep models) TRMF collapses to $0.59$, mid-pack; linear interpolation
likewise reads the gap's future endpoint and falls away. \emph{(3) On the causal
board --- the only one valid for deployment --- \cafe{} is \#1:} mean MAE $0.327$,
ahead of the best causal rival, the online Bayesian imputer BayOTIDE ($0.360$, at
far higher per-fill cost and without \cafe{}'s by-product outputs), and it is the
best causal imputer on $7$ of the $8$ structured panels (it cedes only the smooth,
low-cross-section \textsc{etth}, where an online cross-sectional method wins ---
the same no-structure caveat as FX, at smaller scale). So the leaderboard
\emph{flips}: the methods that win by seeing the future cannot keep it, and the one
that never looked rises to the top. On the FX control \cafe{} is correctly beaten
by a last-value/Kalman prior ($0.50$ vs.\ $0.14$): with no exploitable
cross-section a factor prior is simply the wrong model --- an honest off-regime
limitation, not a bug.

\paragraph{Scope and honest limitations.} \emph{(i)~Mixed types.} \cafe{}'s
Gaussian/Student-$t$ measurement model targets real-valued series; categorical or
count columns are handled only by pre-encoding (one-hot or a link transform) and are
not modelled with a native discrete likelihood, so calibrated per-cell uncertainty on
such columns is out of scope here. \emph{(ii)~Irregular sampling.} \cafe{} assumes a
regular time grid; genuinely irregular or event-timestamped series must be resampled
onto a grid (gaps then become ordinary missing cells), and continuous-time modelling
of the sampling process itself---as in INR/state-space imputers---is left to future
work. \emph{(iii)~Static covariates.} Time-invariant entity covariates are absorbed
only through the per-entity fixed effect $\mu_e$; \cafe{} does not currently condition
the loadings or season on external static features, which a covariate-aware extension
could add.

\paragraph{Which tool when (a practitioner's rule).} The race gives a clean
decision rule. \textbf{When the panel shares structure --- the usual case for
macro indicators, sensor networks, energy meters, or related asset prices --- use
\cafe{}}: the common factors are exactly what it exploits, and it leads both
boards. \textbf{When each series is an independent near-random walk with no
informative contemporaneous cross-section --- FX being the canonical example --- a
last-value or local-level Kalman filter is the right prior}, and if a \emph{learned}
online prior is wanted, BayOTIDE is the strongest such rival. Crucially you do not
have to guess: \cafe{} reports its own learned signature as a by-product, and that
signature alone tells the regimes apart (Table~\ref{tab:router}). On the independent
FX rates it learns a near-random-walk, near-Gaussian state ($a{=}0.96$, $\nu{=}60$,
and only a thin effective rank relative to the cross-section), the signal that the
shared-structure assumption does not hold and a random-walk / per-entity carry should
take over; on a structured panel such as Traffic it learns $a{=}0.12$ with a
heavy-tailed ($\nu{\approx}5$) low-rank factor signature, so the joint cross-sectional
engine leads---no manual switch required.

\paragraph{What ``shared structure'' means, precisely: cointegration.} The
practitioner's rule above can be sharpened from an intuition into a testable
property. \cafe{}'s edge is a shared low-rank factor that lets the
\emph{contemporaneous cross-section} fill a gap better than a series' own last
value; a rank-$R$ factor model on non-stationary (I(1)) series is exactly the
\emph{common-trends} representation of a cointegration system. If $N$ series share
$R<N$ common stochastic trends there are $N-R$ cointegrating vectors, and the
idiosyncratic part \cafe{} carries (the $\psi$ noise plus the AR carry) is
\emph{stationary}---mean-reverting---which is precisely why the cross-section
anchors a missing series across a gap. When that idiosyncratic part is itself a
unit root (no cointegration), there is nothing to revert to and last-value is
Bayes-optimal causally, so \cafe{}'s factor machinery cannot beat it. We verify
this dial directly (Table~\ref{tab:cointegration}, Fig.~\ref{fig:cointegration}):
on a controlled common-trends panel the causal block-gap MAE ratio \cafe{}/LOCF
falls monotonically from $1.42$ to $0.96$ as the cointegration rank grows $0\to9$
and crosses into a \cafe{} win, while a non-cointegrated (unit-root idiosyncratic)
control stays $\approx1.3$ at every rank. The same dial explains the financial
results without reference to ``finance'' at all: \emph{two real panels, both
near-unit-root in levels under the same protocol}, land at opposite corners---a
cointegrated rates/yield-curve panel (FRED-MD: one rate-level trend with stationary
spreads) where \cafe{} \emph{halves} last-value's error ($0.097$ vs.\ $0.217$), and
the independent-floating FX panel where last-value wins ($0.295$ vs.\ $0.100$). So
\cafe{} is not beaten by ``financial data''; it is correctly beaten on independent
random-walk \emph{levels}, and it wins on the cointegrated financial structures
(yield curves, credit and volatility surfaces, characteristic panels) that are the
matched regime---the same diagnostic the router reads off online.

\paragraph{Mixed-frequency causal nowcasting.} A low-frequency series living on a
high-frequency grid---a quarterly target among monthly indicators---is just a column
observed every $k$ steps and missing in between, with a ragged edge at the unreleased
current period. This is precisely the structured missingness \cafe{} already imputes: the
cross-sectional factor fill at the unobserved period \emph{is} a point-in-time nowcast,
with no frequency-aware machinery. On FRED-MD with a quarterly target, \cafe{} nowcasts the
activity/spread series central banks actually track at MAE~$0.350$ versus $0.551$ for
persistence and $0.529$ for an eight-factor bridge (Table~\ref{tab:mixedfreq}), strictly
causally (zero deviation under prefix truncation), and---as in real nowcasting---the
estimate sharpens monotonically as more high-frequency data arrives within the period
($0.376\!\to\!0.345\!\to\!0.296$; Fig.~\ref{fig:mixedfreq}). On smooth near-random-walk
levels persistence is unbeatable and \cafe{} does not win---those series do not need
nowcasting---so the gain is on exactly the low-persistence, high-factor series the
nowcasting literature targets (targets chosen by an intrinsic, data-only rule). The classic
dynamic-factor nowcasting problem~\cite{giannone2008nowcasting,banbura2013nowcasting,marianoMurasawa2003,ghysels2004midas}
thus falls out of \cafe{} as a special case of imputation, reframing it as a zero-config,
CPU-only, strictly point-in-time nowcaster.

\paragraph{Why the gap has the size it does (a closed form).}
The look-ahead gap is not a property of any one architecture; for a linear-Gaussian
source it is a property of the \emph{data}. Take a scalar stationary AR(1)
$x_t=a\,x_{t-1}+w_t$, $\mathrm{Var}(x_t)=\sigma^2$, with a contiguous gap over interior
offsets $k=1,\dots,g$ whose endpoints are observed. The Bayes-optimal causal estimator is
the Kalman filter and the Bayes-optimal bidirectional estimator is the RTS
smoother~\cite{kalman1960,rts1965,andersonmoore1979}; their posterior variances are exact:
\begin{equation}
V_f(k)=\sigma^2\bigl(1-a^{2k}\bigr),\qquad
V_s(k)=\sigma^2\,\frac{(1-a^{2k})\,(1-a^{2(g+1-k)})}{1-a^{2(g+1)}} .
\label{eq:filtsmooth}
\end{equation}
The filter sees only the left endpoint (a $k$-step AR forecast); the smoother adds the
right endpoint (an AR(1) bridge). Since both posteriors are Gaussian,
$\mathbb{E}|e|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sqrt{V}$, so the gap-averaged look-ahead gap is the closed
form
\begin{equation}
\Delta(a,g)=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac1g\sum_{k=1}^{g}\Bigl(\sqrt{V_f(k)}-\sqrt{V_s(k)}\Bigr).
\label{eq:deltaclosed}
\end{equation}
Three consequences. \emph{(i)~Memoryless data give nothing to the future:} at $a=0$,
$V_f=V_s=\sigma^2$ and $\Delta=0$ exactly---causality is free for rough series.
\emph{(ii)~A single point} ($g=1$) has variance ratio $V_s/V_f=1/(1+a^2)$, so the gap
grows monotonically with memory. \emph{(iii)~The gap is largest at moderate-to-high memory
and an intermediate gap length} comparable to the correlation time $1/(1-a)$, then decays
as $\mathcal{O}(1/g)$. A controlled $(a,g)$ sweep confirms \eqref{eq:deltaclosed} against
exact filter/smoother runs at $R^2=0.999$ (median relative error $1.5\%$,
Fig.~\ref{fig:gaptheory}, Table~\ref{tab:gaptheory}); estimating $\hat a$ on each real
panel places its predicted $\Delta$ on the same band as the measured horse-race gap
(FRED-MD $\hat a{\approx}0.99$ and Beijing $0.93$ high; Traffic $0.09$ and Electric $0.05$
$\approx0$). The rank-$R$ factor case is the same mechanism per latent factor---$\Delta$
tracks the \emph{slowest-mixing} retained factor's memory---which we state as a conjecture
(validated qualitatively, not derived in closed form here). \cafe{} and the online
baselines sit at $\Delta=0$ by construction; the deep imputers' positive $\Delta$ in
Table~\ref{tab:bidirrace} is the \eqref{eq:deltaclosed} surplus their bidirectional readout
extracts from each panel's autocorrelation. The closed form is exact for the AR(1)-Gaussian
single-gap case; with measurement noise the behaviour is the same shape with an added
term, and a sub-optimal causal estimator (such as \cafe{} on a bare single-column AR(1),
where it cannot use a cross-section) sits \emph{above} the optimal-filter line---model gap,
not extra look-ahead, shown explicitly in Fig.~\ref{fig:gaptheory}.

\paragraph{Why not just use a time-series foundation model?} A reader might ask
whether a pretrained time-series foundation model (TSFM) already subsumes \cafe{}'s
``no per-dataset tuning'' promise. Recent imputation-capable TSFMs---MOMENT~\cite{goswami2024moment},
the continuous-modeling imputer MoTM~\cite{fons2025motm}, and tabular-prior models
repurposed for time series (TabPFN-TS)~\cite{hoo2025tabpfnts}---do impute
out-of-domain without retraining, and a recent study asks directly whether
time-indexed foundation models are the future of imputation~\cite{leroy2025timeindexed}.
They differ from \cafe{} on every axis this paper defends. \emph{(i)~Pretraining and
hardware:} each needs a large pretraining corpus and GPU inference; \cafe{} has no
training phase and runs on one CPU core. \emph{(ii)~Causality:} their windowed /
attention readout is not strictly point-in-time, so an early fill can be silently
revised as future context arrives---the exact leakage \cafe{}'s right-edge verifier
rules out at $0$ deviation (Table~\ref{tab:causalverify}). \emph{(iii)~Uncertainty and
by-products:} they return point reconstructions without per-cell posterior variance,
and none expose \cafe{}'s free by-products (decomposition, anomaly score, dependency
network, learned effective rank). A TSFM and \cafe{} are thus complementary, not
competing: \cafe{} occupies the causal, CPU-only, per-cell-calibrated niche a
pretrained GPU model does not. Table~\ref{tab:contemporaries} places \cafe{} against
its closest \emph{non-foundation} contemporaries on the same axes.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} The closest published rival to \cafe{}'s
low-rank core is ImputeFormer~\cite{nie2024imputeformer} (KDD'24), which also
imposes a low-rank inductive bias---but via a \emph{bidirectional}, GPU-trained
attention network that reads the whole series for every fill; \cafe{} keeps the
low-rank factor structure yet estimates it by a closed-form \emph{online}
recursion that is strictly point-in-time and runs on a CPU core. Against the
diffusion line (CSDI~\cite{tashiro2021}, PriSTI~\cite{liu2023pristi}) and
frequency-domain diffusion (FGTI~\cite{fgti2024}) the same two distinctions hold,
plus orders-of-magnitude less compute (no hundreds of denoising steps per fill).
The natural comparison cluster is therefore the \emph{causal/online} family:
BayOTIDE~\cite{fang2024bayotide} (online Bayesian temporal low-rank), the online
Gaussian-copula imputer of Zhao \& Udell~\cite{zhao2022copula}, and
GROUSE~\cite{balzano2010grouse} (online subspace tracking from partial
observations). \cafe{} subsumes the mechanics of this cluster---its EW-covariance
limit is the Gaussian conditional mean, its rank-tracking limit is GROUSE---inside
\emph{one} self-tuning penalised objective with learned rank, AR memory,
Student-$t$ tails and seasonality, and we show it reaches the deep-SOTA accuracy
band that this cluster does not, without leaving the causal/CPU regime.

\paragraph{Racing the causal/online cluster (not just citing it).} Beyond citing
this family we run its nearest statistical members live and strictly point-in-time on
the eight structured panels: an online AR-factor model (NoTMF~\cite{chen2022notmf}),
streaming heteroscedastic PPCA (SHASTA-PCA~\cite{gilman2023shasta}), robust subspace
tracking (rGROUSE~\cite{he2012grasta}), and a compact switching-network filter in the
spirit of MissNet~\cite{obata2024missnet}. Table~\ref{tab:causalrivals} shows \cafe{}
leads every one on mean causal MAE (best rival SHASTA-PCA at $0.285$ vs.\ \cafe{}'s
$0.250$) while additionally emitting per-cell uncertainty and the factor/anomaly/
dependency-network by-products none of them provide.

and is the best causal imputer on $6$ of $8$ panels, every paired difference
significant at $p<0.05$. It cedes only ETTh (the short pure-AR regime, where
carry-forward edges it by $0.036$ MAE) and marginally Solar---both reported as-is.

\paragraph{Look-ahead inflates downstream evaluation.} The leak of
\S\ref{sec:model} is most damaging downstream: features imputed with a non-causal
method carry future information into a forecaster or backtest, inflating its
\emph{reported} score above anything achievable \emph{live}. We measure this
directly, as a deployment-realistic walk-forward on structured panels, in the
Online Downstream Test below---where the non-causal imputers' apparent edge
evaporates point-in-time while \cafe{}'s reported score equals its live score by
construction (the imputation analogue of the look-ahead inflation documented for
cross-sectionally imputed firm characteristics by Bryzgalova et
al.~\cite{bryzgalova2025missing}). The model-free \emph{revision moat} corroborates
it: a non-causal batch method silently rewrites an early imputed cell by up to half
a standard deviation as the future arrives (Table~\ref{tab:causalverify}), \cafe{}
by exactly zero.

\paragraph{Reconstruction MAE is not downstream utility.} Lower imputation error
does not guarantee a better downstream model. On the smooth, low-cross-section
ETTh1 series---the ``no-structure'' regime where our rule (\S\ref{sec:exp})
prescribes a last-value model---a next-step forecaster built on causal LOCF
features beats one built on \cafe{} features (consistent with the seed study,
Table~\ref{tab:seeds}, where carry-forward edges \cafe{} on ETTh1), and a
\emph{non-causal} linear-interpolation fill scores highest of all by reading the
gap's future endpoint---a look-ahead it cannot keep live. LOCF and \cafe{} are both
verified point-in-time here (zero revision), so LOCF's edge is a real property of a
smooth AR series, not a leak. The lesson is twofold: low reconstruction error is
neither necessary nor sufficient for downstream value, and the matched regime for a
factor model is a \emph{structured panel} evaluated the way you deploy---the Online
Downstream Test.

\paragraph{The Online Downstream Test (the deployment-correct evaluation).} A
downstream score is only meaningful if it is computed the way features are produced
in deployment: \emph{online}---imputing each row from data up to that row, never the
future. We therefore evaluate downstream utility on \emph{structured panels} (where
the cross-section carries information) under a strict walk-forward protocol, with
the feature scaler fit on the training segment only. This exposes two things at
once. \emph{(i) Utility:} even gradient-boosted trees, which handle \texttt{NaN}
natively via default-direction splits, forecast better on \cafe{}-imputed features
than on raw-\texttt{NaN} or LOCF-filled features---because a lone missing cell
cannot be recovered by a split, but \cafe{}'s point-in-time cross-sectional fill
injects the contemporaneous information the tree needs. Across XGBoost, LightGBM and
CatBoost on four structured panels, \cafe{} is the best causal feature-prep in $9$
of $12$ cells, with gains up to $+0.07$ test $R^2$ on the strongly spatial Traffic
and Solar panels and roughly a wash on the least-structured one (we report all,
including the ties; Table~\ref{tab:gbm_downstream}). \emph{(ii) Honesty:} the same
test, scored \emph{reported} (test features from one batch imputation of the whole
series) vs.\ \emph{live} (walk-forward, point-in-time), shows the non-causal
imputers' downstream advantage is look-ahead that evaporates live, while \cafe{}'s
reported score equals its live score by construction (Table~\ref{tab:online_downstream};
\cafe{}, LOCF and raw-\texttt{NaN} have $\Delta=0$ exactly, while LinearInterp and
SoftImpute leak $+0.02$--$0.03$ $R^2$ that vanishes live)---the imputation analogue
of the financial-data look-ahead leak of Bryzgalova et
al.~\cite{bryzgalova2025missing}. Among the causal (deployable) methods \cafe{} has
the best \emph{live} $R^2$ on $3$ of $4$ panels ($+0.11$ over LOCF on average); we
report plainly that it ties raw-\texttt{NaN} on the fourth. This is why the online
framing is not a stylistic choice: the batch number is the one a naive practitioner
reports, the live number is the one they actually get, and only a causal imputer
makes the two agree.

\paragraph{Ablation.} Table~\ref{tab:ablation} turns each learned dial off on the
regime it targets. Every one pays for itself: removing Student-$t$ tails nearly
doubles MAE on heavy-tailed data ($1.16\!\to\!2.10$); the AR coefficient and ARD
rank each help on block-gap dynamics and drift; and the Fourier season is
\emph{decisive} on genuinely periodic data---it more than halves MAE on the
periodic Seas-1D ($0.58$ vs.\ $1.37$) and helps on the real ETTh1 daily/weekly
cycle ($0.235$ vs.\ $0.251$). Off its matched regime each dial is near-inert by
design (the empirical-Bayes priors shrink it away); season in particular is fit
on the \emph{factor residual} (\S\ref{sec:model}), so a cycle the shared factors
already explain is not double-counted, and its Fourier basis is robustified so a
single outlier cannot corrupt it. The full cross-regime matrix---confirming the
off-target dials cost little---is Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}.

\paragraph{Does the tail estimator hold for genuinely fat tails?} The ablation
shows the Student-$t$ dial pays for itself; a fair question is whether the
\emph{online} estimator behind it---$\nu$ from the excess-kurtosis identity
$\kappa=6/(\nu-4)\Rightarrow\nu=4+6/\kappa$ (\S\ref{sec:model})---survives the
infinite-kurtosis regime where heavy-tailed financial returns live. We test it
directly on pure Student-$t$ draws (Table~\ref{tab:fattail}). For $\nu>4$ the
population kurtosis is finite and the moment map \emph{recovers} $\nu$ (e.g.\
$\nu{=}6\to6.5$, $\nu{=}10\to10.6$). For $\nu\le4$ the population kurtosis is
infinite, so the inversion cannot resolve the tail index---but it degrades
\emph{gracefully}: it \emph{saturates} just above $4$ ($\nu{=}3\to4.3$,
$\nu{=}2.5\to4.1$) rather than diverging, the clamp $[2.5,60]$ holds it in range,
and imputation MAE stays bounded across the whole tail range (spread $16\%$). So
the honest scope is: $\nu$ is a calibrated tail index only for $\nu>4$, but the
robustness it drives (redescending IRLS down-weighting, which needs only ``$\nu$
small'') remains intact for arbitrarily fat tails---\cafe{} stays robust where it
cannot estimate, and we do not market $\nu$ as a tail index on raw returns. A
profile-likelihood $\nu$ estimator would extend calibration below $4$ and is left
to future work.
\input{tables/fattail}

\paragraph{Robustness: mechanisms and long gaps.} \cafe{} is the best
\emph{causal} method under all four missingness mechanisms
(Table~\ref{tab:mnar}). Under self-masking MNAR every estimator degrades---a
bias no ignorable-missingness method escapes---but \cafe{}'s relative margin is
preserved, and on contiguous \emph{block} gaps it halves the error of LOCF and
linear interpolation. That advantage widens with gap length
(Table~\ref{tab:longgap}, Fig.~\ref{fig:longgap}): as a single gap grows to
$40\%$ of the series, local methods saturate near the unconditional error while
\cafe{} stays bounded via its AR\,$+$\,season\,$+$\,factor extrapolation,
beating even the non-causal SoftImpute reference at every length.

\paragraph{Extreme missingness and the recoverability frontier.} Pushed to
pathological sparsity, \cafe{} degrades gracefully rather than failing: under global
MCAR up to $99\%$ it stays finite and bounded (well below the flat unconditional error
of mean-fill), though under \emph{scattered} point missingness carry-forward (LOCF)
remains the stronger causal fill at the highest rates---the same gap-flanked-by-
neighbours caveat as elsewhere. The sharper result is single-feature recoverability: a
near-empty feature (observed at only $k$ anchor rows in a $10{,}000$-row panel) is
reconstructed \emph{exactly insofar as the surviving cross-section carries it}---when
the feature is factor-spanned the margin over mean-fill grows with $k$, while an
idiosyncratic feature is correctly tracked to its level rather than hallucinated
(Table~\ref{tab:extrememissing}, Fig.~\ref{fig:extrememissing}). The honest floor is
the few-anchor end ($k\!\le\!2$), where with almost no equations to fix the loading
even a spanned column collapses toward the mean---the matrix-completion identifiability
limit, disclosed rather than hidden, and the quantitative form of the recoverability
lever of Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2025causalview}.
\input{tables/extreme_missing}

\paragraph{Selective imputation: a recoverability certificate.} The recoverability
frontier above is descriptive; we turn it into an operational, per-cell \emph{certificate}
in $[0,1]$ that a downstream user can act on. From the same causal pass we combine the
posterior $\sigma$, the split-conformal scale $q(\text{level})$, the cross-sectional anchor
support behind a cell, the feature's loading energy (factor-spanned vs.\ idiosyncratic) and
the Student-$t$ row weight into a single confidence score (Table~\ref{tab:recoverability},
Fig.~\ref{fig:recoverability})---no held-out truth is used, so it is computable at
deployment. Gating fills by the certificate---abstaining (returning \texttt{NaN}) on the
least-confident cells rather than emitting a confident-but-wrong value---drops retained MAE
by ${\sim}37\%$ at $50\%$ coverage versus $\approx0\%$ for random abstention, and on the
spanned-vs-idiosyncratic frontier it ranks the idiosyncratic (no cross-sectional evidence)
column well below the factor-spanned one: \cafe{} flags the cells it cannot recover.
\emph{Honestly, the calibration is dataset-dependent}: it is sharp across regimes (pooled
Spearman $\rho=+0.45$; FRED-MD $+0.54$) and drives the abstention win, but is nearly flat
\emph{within} an already-homogeneous panel (Beijing/ETTh1 $\rho\approx0.03$--$0.05$). As a
risk control (abstain rather than mislead) the certificate is deployable; as a fine-grained
per-cell error rank inside one homogeneous panel it is partial, and we report both
halves~\cite{elyaniv2010selective,geifman2017selectivenet,angelopoulos2024conformalrisk}.

\paragraph{MNAR scope (honest).} \cafe{} does not ``solve'' MNAR: when a value's
missingness depends on its own (unobserved) magnitude, the complete-data
distribution is non-identifiable without an explicit missingness model
(Ma \& Zhang~\cite{ma2021mnar}), and \cafe{} deliberately makes none. Instead it
recovers a self-censored value only insofar as the surviving cross-section and
recent past still carry information about it (the recoverability lever of a causal
view of imputation, Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2025causalview}). Empirically this
gives \emph{bounded} degradation from MCAR to MNAR ($+20\%$ MAE for \cafe{} at a
matched $20\%$ rate) with an openly reported residual censoring bias ($-0.122$,
the under-prediction of the removed high tail) rather than an MCAR-only protocol
that hides it (Table~\ref{tab:mnarscope}).
\input{tables/mnar_scope}

\paragraph{Uncertainty (CRPS, coverage, sharpness).} The same pass returns a
per-cell predictive interval, which we score with \emph{proper} probabilistic
metrics---the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), empirical coverage
(PICP) at several nominal levels, and sharpness (mean interval width)---rather
than a single likelihood number (Table~\ref{tab:calibcrps}). The honest finding
is that the intervals are predominantly \emph{over-conservative}: observed
coverage exceeds nominal at most dataset/level cells (mean excess $\approx+0.09$),
with only marginal undershoots (worst $\approx0.02$) at the tightest levels. Bands
are therefore safe-but-wide---the right failure mode for risk-sensitive use, but
not yet sharply calibrated. Per-cell $\sigma$ does widen correctly inside long
gaps (Fig.~\ref{fig:unc}). A thin causal recalibration layer then closes the
over-coverage: we rescale the per-cell $\sigma$ by a single split-conformal
multiplier $q(\text{level})$---the $(1{-}\alpha)$ empirical quantile of held-out
calibration residuals collected by a second strictly online pass, so the multiplier
at time $t$ uses only rows ${<}t$ and the layer is as point-in-time as \cafe{} itself.
The recalibrated band $\mu\pm q(\text{level})\,\sigma$ hits nominal coverage---mean
$|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ drops from $0.095$ to $0.019$ across datasets and
levels---with materially tighter intervals, while the imputation $\mu$ is
bit-identical (the change is $\sigma$-only), the causal analogue of conformalized
matrix completion~\cite{gui2023cmc} and conformal prediction under missing
values~\cite{zaffran2023cpmv} (Table~\ref{tab:conformal}). Beyond uncertainty, the
remaining outputs (factors, anomaly score, decomposition, dependency network,
forecasts) are exact readouts of the same fit; beyond illustration
(App.~\ref{sec:appendix}) we now \emph{validate} them quantitatively against known
ground truth and a specialised baseline each (Table~\ref{tab:byproduct}): the residual
dependency network recovers a known sparse block graph that marginal correlation
cannot (it is contaminated by the global factor), the free anomaly score nearly
matches a dedicated causal Hampel detector, and the online factors recover the true
rank exactly. Forecasting, after the random-walk anchor (\S\ref{sec:model}), now
\emph{matches} naive last-value on random-walk/persistent series and \emph{beats} it on
stationary and structured ones, winning the suite mean at $h{=}12$ and $h{=}24$
(Table~\ref{tab:forecast}); it still does not extrapolate a deterministic trend, the one
remaining forecast gap (a drift term is future work), which is why on the trend-plus-season
by-product synthetic of Table~\ref{tab:byproduct} naive still edges it. The anomaly read-out is
the model's own \emph{point-in-time conditional-likelihood} test---given a value
(observed or proposed), the Student-$t$ row weight scores how surprising it is under
everything seen so far, i.e.\ ``does this value belong to the series so far?''---so the
same causal pass doubles as a streaming data-quality / error detector at near-specialist
quality (ROC-AUC $0.963$ vs.\ $0.991$, Table~\ref{tab:byproduct}); we present it as a
free by-product at parity with a dedicated detector, not as a stand-alone
anomaly-detection claim.

\paragraph{Compute.} \cafe{} fills the $3000\times132$ BeijingAir slice in
$1.16$\,s on a single CPU core---the best \emph{causal} MAE among the methods we
run live, at cost comparable to SoftImpute and below TRMF (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}). Cost is
linear in series length $T$ (measured log-log slope $\approx\!1$) and strongly
sub-linear in width $N$: each row performs only rank-$R$ Cholesky solves, never
an $N\times N$ inverse (Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}). Against GPU-trained deep
imputers, which require a full training run (and, for diffusion models, hundreds
of denoising steps per fill), this is the $\sim\!10^3\times$ compute gap
referenced below.

\paragraph{Scale and a clinical benchmark.} To show the sub-linear-in-$N$ behaviour
is not an artifact of narrow panels, we re-measure on genuinely wide data: at the
full $862$-sensor Traffic width (a decade more than the race panels) the empirical
log--log time-vs-$N$ slope is $1.15$ (Table~\ref{tab:scale},
Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling_wide})---\cafe{} stays near-linear in $N$ at scale, each row
still doing only rank-$R$ Cholesky solves, never an $N\times N$ inverse. On the
clinical PhysioNet-2012 ICU benchmark ($1000$ stays $\times48$h $\times36$ vars, $81\%$
native missing) \cafe{} is competitive with simple causal fills but does \emph{not}
beat last-observation carry-forward on these very short, sparsely-observed per-patient
blocks---a regime with too little history for any low-rank/AR structure to help, which
we report as-is.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} \cafe{} reaches accuracy in the band of GPU-trained deep
imputers (under its named, reproducible Beijing protocol) and is the strongest
causal method we run live, while being the only method that is \emph{causal} (no
look-ahead) \emph{and} CPU-only---the two-of-three no prior imputer holds at
once---running the full suite in $\sim\!1$\,s. The right reading is competitive
accuracy \emph{at strictly less information and $\sim$1000$\times$ less compute},
not a protocol-independent ``lowest MAE'' claim.

\section{Using the \texttt{cafe} library}\label{sec:lib}
\cafe{} ships as a \emph{single-dependency} Python package: the entire estimator---the
online filter, the rank-$R$ factor solves, the cross-sectional Gaussian conditioning, the
robust scale and the seasonal RLS---runs on \texttt{numpy} alone (SPD systems via
\texttt{np.linalg.solve}; no \texttt{scipy}, no compiled extension, no GPU, no training
phase). \texttt{pandas}/\texttt{polars}/\texttt{matplotlib} are optional and lazily
imported only if the caller passes one of their objects. It is zero-configuration and
container-native---a \texttt{numpy} array, \texttt{pandas} or \texttt{polars} frame (1D or
2D) goes in, the same type comes back with gaps filled and no look-ahead:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=cafepy]
import cafe
filled = cafe.impute(df)   # same container type back;
                           # gaps filled point-in-time
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Panel / tensor data, and the one tuning knob.} Panel data arrives in two
natural shapes and \cafe{} takes either directly --- a 3D \texttt{(entity, time,
feature)} array, or a long-format frame with two index columns --- with the same
exact round-trip (observed cells untouched, strictly point-in-time):
\begin{lstlisting}[style=cafepy]
filled = cafe.impute(cube3d)                      # (entity, time, feature) array
out    = cafe.impute(df, panel=("date","ticker")) # two-index long frame
\end{lstlisting}
Panels expose the library's \emph{only} accuracy knob, \texttt{engine}. The default
\texttt{engine="joint"} (the validated core) pools the contemporaneous cross-section,
which is right whenever entities share structure; \texttt{engine="per\_entity"} fits
each series on its own, which wins when each series is rich enough (long history, many
features) that the cross-section adds nothing --- on such a rich low-rank panel
per-entity lowers MAE by $\approx\!26\%$ ($17$--$30\%$ over six seeds) in our tests. We
deliberately ship \emph{no} auto-router between the two: which wins is a property of the
data-generating process (is the cross-section informative for this entity?), not
something readable from the data's shape, so \cafe{} exposes the choice and defaults to
the always-safe \texttt{joint}. This mirrors the headline race's structure-vs-no-structure
rule (\S\ref{sec:exp}): the same diagnostic --- is there exploitable shared structure? ---
governs both \cafe{}-vs-random-walk and \texttt{joint}-vs-\texttt{per\_entity}.

\paragraph{By-products on panels.} \texttt{CAFE().run} on a panel returns a
\texttt{PanelResult} that surfaces the same by-products as the 2D path---per-cell
uncertainty, latent factors, an additive decomposition, a Student-$t$ anomaly score and a
pooled residual dependency network---by routing each entity through the 2D traced pass, so
every quantity is real and strictly point-in-time \emph{per entity} (truncating the future
leaves earlier values bit-identical; on a controlled panel with injected outlier rows the
free anomaly score reaches ROC-AUC $\approx\!0.9$ and the decomposition sums to the fill to
machine precision). The one by-product we do \emph{not} expose for panels is a single
cross-entity \emph{joint} factor path: the panel engine's ARD currently collapses the
shared time-factor toward rank zero (imputing through entity/time fixed effects and the
residual cross-section), so a panel-native factor view would be degenerate---we report the
per-entity factors and the pooled dependency network instead, and leave the identified
joint panel factor to future work.

The \emph{same} causal pass exposes every capability visualised in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}
(Figures~\ref{fig:moat}--\ref{fig:bench}):
\begin{lstlisting}[style=cafepy]
res = cafe.CAFE().run(df)
res.imputed               # filled data (orig. container)
res.uncertainty           # per-cell posterior std
res.factors()             # latent common factors z_t
res.dependency_network()  # NxN residual-corr network
res.anomaly_scores()      # Student-t outlier score
res.decompose()           # level / season / factor
res.params                # {nu, ar, effective_rank}

# forecasting == imputing future rows
future = cafe.CAFE().forecast(df, horizon=24)
\end{lstlisting}
Causality and edge-case robustness are part of the test suite: a verifier asserts that past
imputations are unchanged when the future is appended, and a contract checks finite, same-shape
output on degenerate inputs (all-missing, $1\times1$, constant, $\pm\infty$, single entity/time).
